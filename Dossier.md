# Benson Dossier - Draft 1 (Courts, Conflicts, Connections)

As of Oct 27, 2026

## Scope and rating key

This draft covers Jocelyn Benson, Michigan Secretary of State and the 2026 Democratic nominee for governor (general election November 3, 2026, opponent John James). It looks at court cases naming her, ethics and conflict of interest questions, and claims tying her to racial hate groups. Public records and published reporting only. No addresses, family details or private individuals.

- **Documented:** a court record, official filing or government release, or the same fact confirmed by several credible outlets.
- **Reported:** one outlet, or an outlet with a stake in the race. A lead, not proof.
- **Disputed:** credible sources conflict.
- **Not Supported:** claimed, but nothing on the current record backs it.

A lawsuit filing is Documented as a filing. What the plaintiff alleges is not established until a court says so. Partisan outlets on either side (Gateway Pundit, The Midwesterner, Enjoyer, The Federalist, party press releases) are used only to find leads and are rated Reported at best.

## Bottom line

The record supports several real accountability questions. It does not support a direct tie between Benson and any racial hate group.

1. **No direct hate group tie found. Not Supported.** The claim runs through the Southern Poverty Law Center, where Benson sat on the board from 2014 to about 2018 or 2019. Her board service is Documented. The federal indictment of the SPLC does not name her, and no source shows she knew of the payments at issue.
2. **Active cases mostly challenge her office's election rules and records.** Live matters include a challenge to her seven-day poll book deletion rule, a public records suit over SPLC communications, a ballot eligibility suit, and two racial discrimination suits against her department. The DOJ voter roll suit was dismissed and the dismissal was affirmed on appeal.
3. **A state finding exists against her. Documented.** The Attorney General's office found she violated the Campaign Finance Act by announcing her campaign in a state building in January 2025. The office has no power to penalize the Secretary of State, so no penalty followed.
4. **Her husband's pay is the sharpest conflict question. Documented pay, Disputed meaning.** Her May 2026 disclosure shows $370,000 in 2025 from Related Companies, the developer behind the Saline Township data center. The company says he earned no commission on it and has moved off Michigan work.
5. **Her unusual position as both election administrator and candidate is real. Documented.** She adopted a written recusal "firewall" in May 2026. Critics say it does not fully cure the conflict.

### McQueer v. Benson: what each side argues

Based on four filings read directly on Oct 27: the court's Oct 27 order, the defendants' Oct 7 brief with the Brater affidavit, the plaintiff's Oct 9 amended motion, and the plaintiff's Oct 14 response. Not yet reviewed: the Sept 22 opinion, the Sept 30 declaratory ruling, the exhibits, and the United States' statement of interest. Each side's position below is Documented as that side's argument. Neither is established until the court rules.

- **Plaintiff and the RNC and Michigan GOP:** Rule 168.48 orders clerks to delete electronic poll book files seven days after certification. Federal law (52 USC 20701) and state law (MCL 168.811) require preserving election records and poll lists for 22 months. They say no clerk can obey both. In the alternative, they say the notice of rulemaking never disclosed the deletion command. The plaintiff says the poll book file holds data, including the time of each action and the inspector, that the voter database upload and its reports do not reproduce. The plaintiff's attorney of record is Kevin Kijewski.
- **Benson, Director Brater and the Department of State (her side):** The Department's Sept 30 declaratory ruling concludes the rule is consistent with both statutes. Their position is that the records the law requires are kept elsewhere: the paper application to vote, the printed paper poll book, and the poll book data uploaded to the Qualified Voter File. They describe the deleted item as a working snapshot of the voter list loaded on precinct laptops. They also argue the court lacks jurisdiction because review of a declaratory ruling belongs in a circuit court administrative appeal, and that the rule followed the Administrative Procedures Act.
- **The factual question the case turns on:** whether the upload to the voter database preserves everything the federal statute covers. The plaintiff cites a Department official's Oct 1 affidavit that the Bureau can pull poll book data through voter database reports, and says the reports she ran show no time of day or user. That is her characterization. The defendants' filing was not tested on this point here.
- **A separate federal case:** the plaintiff's brief cites Davis v. Benson, No. 2:26-cv-13454 (E.D. Mich.), where the state also defended the rule. The brief says a motion was heard Oct 7 and withdrawn Oct 11. Reported from one party's brief; not verified on that docket.

Next pulls: the Sept 22 opinion and Sept 30 ruling, the Brater and Fracassi affidavits in full, the United States' statement of interest, and the E.D. Mich. docket.

## Ethics and conflicts of interest

Five items are worth tracking. Only the first is an official finding against her.

**1. State building used for a campaign launch. Documented.** The Attorney General's office told Benson in May 2025 that she personally violated the Michigan Campaign Finance Act by announcing her run in the lobby of the Austin Building on January 22, 2025. Her campaign committee was cleared. The office said it has no authority to impose penalties on the Secretary of State. Her side: she said the lobby was used because of cold weather, and a campaign finance attorney quoted at the time said he knew of no precedent making that a violation.

**2. Husband's employment with a developer before the state. Documented pay, Disputed meaning.** Ryan Friedrichs is a vice president at Related Companies, parent of Related Digital, which is developing the $43 billion Saline Township data center. He held a Michigan lobbying registration for Related until May 2025. Benson's May 2026 financial disclosure shows he earned $370,000 from Related in 2025. His work on the project ran from early summer 2025 to February 2026, and in March 2026 he moved to a national role with no Michigan projects. Related and her campaign say he will recuse from any state business if she is elected, and Related says he earned no commission on the project. Michigan Advance reported that Related is owned by one of her larger donors. A super PAC backing John James says the household profited; Bridge Michigan's fact check treats that claim as contested. Whether pay from the parent company amounts to a conflict is a judgment call. The facts are not in dispute.

**3. Administering an election she is running in. Documented.** Twenty two Republican legislators asked the Justice Department to supervise Michigan's elections, calling this an unavoidable conflict. In May 2026 Benson adopted a written firewall policy, developed with the Election Reformers Network, recusing herself from decisions that directly affect the governor's race. Canvassers have disputed that she could influence signature verification.

**4. Bar complaints over her PAC and a ballot decision. Filed Documented, outcome not found.** In October 2024 Michigan GOP chair Pete Hoekstra complained to the Attorney Grievance Commission that her Michigan Legacy PAC gave $82,500 to Justice Kyra Harris Bolden in April 2024 while a Michigan GOP case against Benson was before the court. Former Secretary of State Ruth Johnson filed a separate complaint alleging Benson misrepresented a ballot deadline to courts in the Robert F. Kennedy Jr. matter. Neither filing proves misconduct, and no result was found for either.

**5. Workplace discrimination claims and a reported settlement. Filing Documented, settlement Reported.** Several Black current and former employees allege discrimination and retaliation in her department (see Court cases). Former Assistant Secretary of State Heaster Wheeler swore that he raised concerns with Benson and she did not act. Her department calls the claims false. The Midwesterner and a Detroit opinion column report a settlement of about $775,000 to $800,000 last summer with former customer service director Angela Harness. No primary record of that settlement was found.

## Hate group connection claims

No source reviewed shows Benson associating with, funding or taking support from a racial hate group. Every version of the claim runs through the Southern Poverty Law Center, an organization whose work is to monitor hate groups and which federal prosecutors now accuse of improper payments to people inside them.

| Claim | Rating | What the record shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benson served on the SPLC board | Documented | Board member from 2014 while dean of Wayne State Law. End date Disputed: Bridge Michigan says she left after her November 2018 election; The Center Square says early 2019; her office said in March 2019 she was stepping down, and PJ Media reported her still listed afterward. She also worked for the SPLC earlier in her career and writes about it in her 2025 memoir. |
| The SPLC was indicted | Documented | A federal grand jury indictment announced in April 2026 charges money laundering and wire fraud. Prosecutors allege more than $3 million went from 2014 to 2023 to people tied to groups including the Ku Klux Klan, Aryan Nations and the National Socialist Movement. Former SPLC intelligence director Heidi Beirich, who served while Benson was on the board, faces added charges. The SPLC denies wrongdoing. Nothing is proven. |
| Benson is named or accused in the indictment | Not Supported | She is not mentioned in the indictment or in public Justice Department statements, per Bridge Michigan. |
| Benson knew of, approved or oversaw the payments | Not Supported | No document or testimony found. A fact check by Media Bias Fact Check rated the Michigan House Republican claim of her involvement with the SPLC while it funded the KKK as Misleading, because the allegations concern payments to informants inside extremist groups, not direct support to the Klan. |
| A board member bears responsibility for oversight failures | Disputed | Nonprofit board members generally owe fiduciary duties (National Council of Nonprofits, as cited by Michigan Advance). Whether that duty was breached depends on what the board was told, which is not public. |
| She kept ties to the SPLC after taking office | Documented email, Disputed meaning | Department emails released to House Oversight show she helped arrange a 2019 tour of SPLC offices for the National Association of Secretaries of State. Republicans call this a continuing working relationship. Her department declined to characterize it. |
| She is a "star operative" who "sent millions" to the KKK (James statement; America Next ad) | Disputed | Contradicted in substance by the fact checks above. |

Her response: she has called the prosecution political and retaliatory, and her campaign says she has spent her career helping dismantle white supremacist and neo-Nazi networks.

Accuracy note for posting: the supportable statement is that she sat on the board of an organization now under federal indictment for payments to extremist group members, and has not publicly explained what the board knew. "Funded the KKK" and "she funded" go beyond the record.
